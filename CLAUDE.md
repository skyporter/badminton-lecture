# CLAUDE.md

Static TypeScript web quiz (Vite, Vitest) to learn reading badminton singles play. No server, no backend, no external API, no AI. UI text is in French, with a non-breaking space (U+00A0) before `?`, `!`, `:` and `;`. Code, names and commits are in English. No explanatory comments in code, full explicit names.

## Commands

- `npm test`: run all tests (`vitest run`).
- `npx tsc --noEmit`: type check.
- `npm run build`: `tsc` then `vite build` into `dist/`.
- `npm run dev`: dev server.

Deployment: GitHub Pages at https://skyporter.github.io/badminton-lecture/. `.github/workflows/deploy.yml` runs the tests, builds and publishes `dist/` on every push to `main`. `vite.config.ts` sets `base: './'` so the built site works under any sub-path.

## Architecture

- `src/engine/`: pure, deterministic logic. Same situation and same level give the same result, bit for bit. `rules.ts` holds every rule, weight and threshold (including the zone rules `ZONE_VALUE_RATIO`, `ZONE_MAXIMUM_DISTANCE`, `ZONE_MINIMUM_RADIUS`): all values live in `rules.ts` and nowhere else, except the shot catalogue in `shots.ts` (flight times, landing depths, playable zones), which is also part of the rules and must be reviewed together with `rules.ts` when tuning. Other files: `court.ts` (geometry), `shots.ts` (shot catalogue), `generator.ts` (seeded situations), `evaluate.ts` and `analysis.ts` (value map, best return, alternative, score), `recovery.ts`, `status.ts`, `explain.ts` (French explanations), `rng.ts`, `types.ts`.
- `src/ui/`: rendering and input only. `app.ts` (state, `nextState` reducer, wiring), `panel.ts` (top bar controls, shot banner, shot popover, reveal drawer), `courtView.ts` (SVG court and players, shared `svgElement` and `SVG_NAMESPACE`), `trajectory.ts` (trajectory code and shared colours), `situationUrl.ts` (`?seed=&level=` read and write), `input.ts` (pointer to court coordinates), `styles.css`.
- `tests/engine/`, `tests/ui/`, `tests/calibration/` (reference situations with expected results; re-run after any change in `rules.ts`).

## Coordinates and players

Court coordinates are in metres: origin top left, x from 0 to 6.10, y from 0 to 13.40. B is at the top (y < 6.70), A at the bottom (y > 6.70), net at y = 6.70. Singles tramlines are at x = 0.46 and x = 5.64. The UI never stores pixels: the cross, the preview and the map are in court coordinates, so they stay aligned after a resize or rotation.

Both players are right-handed (`handedness: 'droitier'` is a parameter, only that value is implemented). A faces -y, so her forehand is at +x. B faces +y, so his forehand is at -x. Handedness is not drawn on the court (no racket, arc or badge). It follows A to the impact point and B to its recovered position in the reveal. In the reveal, A's starting position and B's hitting position are dashed ghosts, and light footprints (sole and heel, alternating, pointing the way they walk, `footsteps` in `courtView.ts`) lead from each ghost to the player's current disc.

## Colours and trajectory code

A `#7cc4ff` (sky blue, chosen for contrast on the green court), B `#e05252`. B's shot is a solid line in B's colour landing on a dot in A's colour; the student's return is dashed in A's colour, ending on a dot in A's colour; the best return is dashed `#ffffff` (with a white dot at its landing point), unreachable part of the student's return `#ff5252` dotted (when the chosen shot cannot land at the cross depth, the blue trajectory stops at `reachableLanding` from `evaluate.ts` and « Ton choix » opens with « Coup impossible jusque-là. »). No heat map: the reveal draws two ovals on B's half, clipped to the singles half of B (`analyse` returns `bestZone` and `alternativeZone`): zone 1 `#00e676` around the best return, zone 2 `#b2ff59` around the second choice (best shot of another family, the « Deuxième choix » line). Each oval is centred on the return's point and covers the cells within 1.5 m where the same shot keeps 90 % of its value; its HTML tag reads « Meilleur retour 1 » / « Meilleur retour 2 » on the first line and the shot name on the second, 0.45 m below the zone point (above it when that would cross the net), so it never covers the white dot.

Trajectories (top view, `trajectory.ts`, which also holds the player colours): solid for B's shot, `STROKE_PIXELS.returnDashArray` for every return of A; there is no speed code anymore. Only the curvature still varies with the shot (`trajectoryCurvature`: straight = flat, slight curve = low arc, strong curve = high arc).

Stroke widths and dash lengths are constant in screen pixels, whatever the zoom or court size: every stroke in the court SVG carries `vector-effect="non-scaling-stroke"` through `constantStroke(...)` and its width comes from `STROKE_PIXELS` (`trajectory.ts`). Only shapes (discs, landing dot, point markers) scale with the court. Each situation writes `?seed=<integer>&level=<level>`, plus `&shot=<shot id>&x=<metres>&y=<metres>` once the answer is validated (the target is rounded to the centimetre in `set-target`, so a reload restores the exact same score; on load and on `popstate` a valid choice restores the reveal through `restore-situation`): « Nouvelle situation » and « Valider » add a history entry (`history.pushState`, so back from a reveal shows the same situation unanswered), every other change (level, answer) replaces the current one (`history.replaceState`). The same URL on load restores the situation, and browser back/forward (`popstate`, action `restore-situation`) shows the situation of the address bar, as a question or as the reveal when the address carries a choice.

On a serve, the receiver A always stands at the usual receiving spot (`RECEIVER_SERVE_DEPTH` 3 m from the net, `RECEIVER_SERVE_CENTER_OFFSET` 0.5 m from the centre line, in the diagonal box); only the side changes with the server's score.

## Layout

Everything happens on the court, on mobile and desktop alike: no side panel. Top to bottom: a thin top bar (`.controls`: level select and « Nouvelle situation »), then the court frame (`.court-frame`, sized from the viewport only) with its overlays. `.shot-banner` (top of the frame) shows `describeShotB` in both phases (« Situation : B fait un amorti rapide en ligne, … »). In the question phase, `.court-hint` (bottom right of the frame, small light text with no background, not clickable) says « Clique sur le terrain de B pour choisir ton retour ». It never covers the court: the SVG viewBox top margin is `BANNER_RESERVED_METRES` (`courtView.ts`, 0.75 m: exactly the two lines every situation text takes, plus padding) plus 0.4 m of out zone, the banner height is that reservation as a percentage of the viewBox height, and its font is in `cqw` (the frame is a size container), so it keeps the same size in metres at any width; B's back line and 0.4 m of out zone stay visible and clickable. The frame width uses `--court-aspect` (viewBox width / height). A click or tap inside the singles half of B places the target point and opens `.shot-popover` next to it (presses on A's half, the tramlines or behind B's back line are ignored by `set-target`, and the hover preview only shows inside that half): the three shot groups (each button has a `title` tooltip from `SHOT_DESCRIPTIONS` in `explain.ts`, and the selected shot's description is also written under the buttons for touch screens), « Valider » (disabled until a shot is chosen) and a close « ✕ ». The popover is positioned in percentages from court coordinates (`--anchor-x`, `--anchor-y`), flips left on the right half (`.to-left`) and up on the lower half (`.upward`), and is clamped inside the frame. Escape (one `document` keydown listener, replaced on each `startApp`) or « ✕ » closes it (the cross stays); clicking the court again moves the cross and reopens it. On touch, dragging moves the preview and the popover opens on release. After validation `.reveal-drawer` overlays the bottom of the frame: collapsed, one line « Score : NN / 100 — Meilleur : <shot> » and a chevron; expanded, the full reveal content (`.choice-box` with a white border: the student's choice, score and its reasons; `.best-box` with a light background, no border and a bright green title: the best return and its reasons; then the « Alternative » line), scrollable, at most 60 % of the court height. It opens expanded right after validation (and when a reload restores a choice); the chevron collapses it. Popover and drawer state live in `QuizState` (`popoverOpen`, `drawerExpanded`, actions `close-popover` and `toggle-drawer`), so `nextState` stays pure; a level change after the reveal keeps the drawer state.

`.trajectory-tags` (HTML, sibling of the SVG, no pointer events) puts the shot name in the middle of each drawn trajectory (`trajectoryMiddle`), positioned in percentages of the viewBox and styled as small dark translucent tags: B's shot in the question phase, the student's return once a shot is chosen, and in the reveal the student's return plus « Meilleur retour 1 » and « Meilleur retour 2 », each with the shot name on a second line, on the zones. The bottom right hint is plain white text with no shadow. There is no legend: the tags on the court name every shot. The reveal drawer sits in `.bottom-stack`. Overlays are siblings of the court SVG, so pointer events on the banner, popover or drawer never reach the court input.
